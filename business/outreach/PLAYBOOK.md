## Demo policy — strict gate

A free preview is an offer, not an existing product.

1. In first outreach, offer a free, no-obligation demo.
2. Build nothing until the business clearly says it wants to see a preview (for example, “yes,” “sure,” or “send me a demo”).
3. Once confirmed, get the few facts needed to make the preview accurate: preferred phone/address/hours, services, booking/menu link, photos or logo, and any must-have content.
4. Hand off as `DEMO CONFIRMED` for a client-branded build. Do not use Lewis Digital’s gold brand on a client demo.
5. Never say a preview is ready until there is a checked, working demo URL.

There is no obligation with a preview. Nothing goes live without the client’s approval.

## Objection handling

### “It’s too expensive.”

**Aim:** acknowledge it; explain the actual price and what it includes; let them see value before deciding. Do not discount.

> I understand. I do not want you to pay for something that does not make sense for the business. The basic site is $299 to build, then $25 a month for hosting and care, including backups, updates, and small edits. The bundle is $399 to set up and $149 a month if having an AI receptionist would save you time answering common questions.
>
> You can still look at the free preview first and decide whether it would be useful. No pressure either way.

If their stated issue is that a site alone will not save enough time, explain the Bundle as the better-value choice for a site plus AI receptionist — never as a price concession. If they cannot afford it now, close graciously or invite them to come back when timing changes.

### “I don’t have time.”

**Aim:** reduce their effort, not invent a timeline.

> I get that. I can keep the preview simple and work from the public details you already have. You can look at it whenever you have a minute, and there is no call or decision required just to see it. If now is not a good time, I’m happy to leave it there.

For a confirmed client, ask only for the one or two missing facts that matter. Do not send a long questionnaire.

### “How does it work?”

**Aim:** explain the steps plainly.

> First, you tell me you want to see a preview. I put together a simple sample in your business’s own style and send you a link. Nothing goes live and there is no charge for that preview. If you like it, we make any final changes, choose the plan that fits, and get it ready for your approval before launch.

Then give prices only if useful: Basic is $299 + $25/month; Bundle is $399 + $149/month. Mention that Google Business Profile setup/optimization can be included where needed and reviews/business details can be shown on the site.

### “We already have a website.”

**Aim:** respect the answer; do not try to create a problem that has not been stated.

> That is good to hear. I do not want to replace something that is working for you. If you ever want help keeping it updated, want a second set of eyes on it, or want an AI receptionist for common questions, I would be glad to help. Otherwise, I’ll leave you to it.

If they ask for a review, inspect only publicly available facts and make specific, verifiable suggestions. Do not claim their site is broken or poorly ranked without evidence.

### “Not interested,” “remove me,” or “no thanks.”

**Aim:** honor it immediately.

> No problem at all. Thanks for letting me know. I’ll close this out and won’t follow up again.

Mark `CLOSED — OPTED OUT` in the log. Do not re-enroll or contact them through another email address.

### “Let me think about it” / “Ask me later.”

**Aim:** give control to the lead.

> Of course. Take the time you need. Would you rather I leave it there, or is there a month that would be better for me to check back in?

Only schedule a later follow-up if they give permission or an actual time. Log the requested month/date; do not send generic cadence emails before then.

### “Can you guarantee more customers / Google rankings?”

**Aim:** be transparent.

> I cannot honestly guarantee a certain number of customers or a Google ranking. What I can do is give people a clear, trustworthy place to find your business information and contact you, and help make sure your Google Business Profile is set up well if you need that.

## When to escalate or pause

Route to the lead/owner for an explicit decision before replying if the prospect asks for:

- A discount, special price, payment plan, free month, or custom package outside the stated offers.
- Legal, privacy, accessibility, hosting-transfer, domain-ownership, contract, refund, or tax terms.
- A guarantee about leads, rankings, revenue, uptime, or performance.
- A timeframe that cannot be confirmed with the Builder.
- A service, integration, or AI capability not already in the approved offer.

Pause outreach immediately for opt-outs, complaints, wrong-recipient reports, or any message that indicates the lead does not want contact.

## Required log discipline

Use `leads/outreach/` as the per-lead record. For every material reply, record:

- date and sender/recipient
- exact customer intent or objection
- confirmed plan or next step
- demo status (`not offered`, `offered`, `confirmed`, `building`, `ready`, `approved`)
- payment link sent, if any
- whether the recurring invoice handoff is needed
- requested follow-up date or clear stop status

This keeps Outreach, Negotiator, Builder, and Payment Agent from making contradictory promises.
